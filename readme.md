We observe that model performance varies considerably across different random seeds, even under identical training configurations. Specifically, we trained models using both the starter notebook provided on Brightspace and the Kaggle web interface, and found that test accuracy can differ by more than $1\%$ depending solely on the seed used for weight initialization and data shuffling.

This sensitivity is particularly pronounced in low-capacity models such as LoRA-tuned architectures, where the number of trainable parameters is limited and optimization trajectories are more susceptible to early-stage variance. Due to the low-rank constraint imposed by LoRA (e.g., $r=4$ or $8$), the model’s capacity to recover from suboptimal initialization is significantly restricted. As a result, favorable seeds that produce more effective weight initializations or early batch compositions can lead to better convergence and generalization.

These findings highlight the importance of reporting results across multiple random seeds, or at least fixing the seed when comparing configurations. They also suggest that future work on parameter-efficient finetuning should pay closer attention to initialization strategies and training stability.
